# Data provenance and numerical evidence

## Public topology sources

The study uses ten structures from Matthew Roughan's Internet Topology Zoo repository, pinned to commit `51118849b99534f01010d885d66321433a7eef9e`. Unmodified GraphML files, source URLs and hashes are retained under `data/InternetTopologyZoo/` and `dis_extension/data_external/`.

Attribution: S. Knight, H. X. Nguyen, N. Falkner, R. Bowden and M. Roughan, *The Internet Topology Zoo*, IEEE Journal on Selected Areas in Communications 29(9), 1765–1775, 2011. https://doi.org/10.1109/JSAC.2011.111002

Source: https://github.com/mroughan/InternetTopologyZoo . The upstream Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 licence is included. This third-party data licence does not automatically license the original project code.

## Generated workloads and recorded execution

Routes, capacities, utility weights and capacity shocks are generated by the supplied numerical code. They are not observed operator traffic. Seeds, parameters, fitted models, raw timings, failures, source hashes, original environment records and saved allocations are retained in the data archive.

The original benchmark records 600 updates and 7,200 timings. The topology-held-out replay records 768 updates and 13,824 timings. Frozen-policy validation records 256 new updates and 9,216 timings across two BLAS configurations on one CPU.

Recorded execution chronology is preserved. Checkpoints support the continuation audit; the interrupted collection with inconsistent BLAS limits remains excluded from training and reported performance. Changing document files does not change the numerical source or observations. Source-text revisions already made during the study are documented in `results/source_text_revision.json` and checked by the audit.

## Interpretation

Repeated timings measure execution noise, rather than independent networks. Synthetic workloads, a limited topology collection and one CPU constrain external inference. The numerical audits check Float64 consistency and source identity. They do not establish publication priority, interval-arithmetic guarantees or independent replication.
